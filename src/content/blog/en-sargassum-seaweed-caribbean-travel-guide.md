# Sargassum Seaweed in the Caribbean: Essential Guide for Travelers & Beachgoer Tips

Since 2011, recurrent influxes of pelagic *Sargassum* (primarily *S. fluitans* and *S. natans*) have transformed coastlines across the wider Caribbean, Gulf of Mexico, and West Africa. While the open-ocean weed serves as a vital ecological nursery for sea turtles and pelagic fish, decomposing mats stranded on beaches pose major challenges.

---

## 1. Seasonal Cycles: When Does Sargassum Arrive?
- **Peak Arrival Window**: Typically between **April and September**, driven by warm tropical Atlantic currents, the North Equatorial Recirculation, and nutrient discharges from the Amazon and Orinoco rivers.
- **Low-Risk Window**: From **November to February**, winter trade winds and cooler sea surface temperatures significantly diminish nearshore rafts.

## 2. Geography: Atlantic vs. Caribbean Coastlines
Due to prevailing easterly trade winds (*Alizés*):
- **Windward / Atlantic Coasts**: Heavily exposed to incoming drift (e.g., eastern Grande-Terre in Guadeloupe, Atlantic Martinique, Riviera Maya).
- **Leeward / Caribbean Coasts**: Naturally sheltered. The western, calm Caribbean waters (such as Bouillante, Deshaies, and the Anses-d'Arlet in Martinique) remain crystal-clear and virtually sargassum-free year-round.

## 3. Practical Advice for Vacationers
1. **Choose Accommodations Wisely**: If visiting during summer, favor hotels or villas situated on western-facing coasts or enclosed bays equipped with deflecting offshore barriers.
2. **Consult Live Webcams & Satellite Maps**: Check daily local municipal advisories and satellite stranding bulletins before heading out for a beach day.
3. **Respect Cleaning Operations**: Coastal tractors and marine harvest boats operate at dawn; keep clear of active collection zones.
