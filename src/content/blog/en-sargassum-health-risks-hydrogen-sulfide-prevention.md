# Hydrogen Sulfide (H2S) & Health Risks: Staying Safe Near Decomposing Sargassum

When floating sargassum washes ashore, anaerobic decomposition begins within 24 to 48 hours. Microorganisms break down the sulfur-rich biomass, releasing volatile gases that can impact human respiratory systems and corrode metals.

---

## Toxic Gas Emissions: H2S & NH3
1. **Hydrogen Sulfide (H2S)**: Characterized by an unmistakable "rotten egg" smell. At low concentrations, it causes olfactory annoyance; at higher levels, it triggers neuro-respiratory symptoms.
2. **Ammonia (NH3)**: Produces pungent, acrid odors and ocular irritation.

## Symptoms of Acute Exposure
Residents and beachgoers exposed downwind to high concentrations may experience:
- Headaches and lightheadedness
- Throat irritation and coughing
- Nausea and loss of appetite
- Eye redness, stinging, and conjunctival discomfort

## Official Safety Guidelines & Thresholds
Health authorities (including the French High Council for Public Health - HCSP) have defined precise air-quality thresholds:
- **< 0.07 ppm**: Normal background level.
- **0.07 to 1.0 ppm**: Olfactory discomfort; sensitive individuals (asthmatics, infants, pregnant women) should avoid prolonged exposure.
- **> 1.0 ppm**: Evacuation of immediate shoreline areas and rapid mechanical clearance mandated.

### Safety Precautions
- Never swim directly within thick decaying brown water mats.
- Keep windows closed if living directly on a downwind bay experiencing fresh strandings.
- Use certified H2S personal detectors if working on municipal collection crews.
